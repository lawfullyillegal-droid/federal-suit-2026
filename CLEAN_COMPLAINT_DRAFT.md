### I. INTRODUCTION & NATURE OF THE ACTION
1. This is a civil rights action brought under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), 15 U.S.C. § 1681, seeking declaratory relief, injunctive relief, and damages. 
2. Plaintiff Travis Ryle challenges a systemic, long-term administrative failure by Mohave County municipal nodes where a valid judicial determination of a misdemeanor disposition (Ventura County, 2007) was continuously misclassified and disseminated across law enforcement and consumer reporting data networks as an active felony status for a period of 144 months (2008–2020).
3. Plaintiff further alleges that consumer reporting agencies Equifax and LexisNexis willfully and negligently failed to maintain reasonable procedures to ensure the maximum possible accuracy of public record information disseminated about Plaintiff, continuing to report inaccurate warrant data and extinguished debts after receiving explicit statutory notice of the discrepancies.

### II. JURISDICTION AND VENUE
4. This Court has federal question jurisdiction pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1331, as this action arises under the Constitution and laws of the United States.
5. Venue is proper in the District of Arizona under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b) because the primary municipal defendants reside in this district and a substantial part of the events giving rise to the claims occurred in Mohave County, Arizona.

### III. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

#### Count A: The 12-Year Administrative Misclassification Gap
6. In 2007, a final judicial disposition was formally entered recording a misdemeanor status regarding Plaintiff’s records. 
7. From 2008 through 2020, administrative database nodes managed and updated by municipal agencies failed to reconcile their reporting logs with the underlying judicial decree, resulting in a persistent 12-year misclassification that erroneously identified Plaintiff as a convicted felon.
8. This structural data reporting error served as the direct proximate cause of subsequent enforcement anomalies, including an invalid warrant flag utilized during an enforcement stop in Kingman, Arizona, in April 2024.

#### Count B: Widespread Ministerial Record Omissions (Monell Custom and Policy)
9. A forensic technical audit of the Mohave County public judicial records conducted on May 13, 2026, identified 1,068 independent instances where the non-discretionary, mandatory ministerial duty to execute and enter a "Return of Service" under Arizona Rule of Criminal Procedure 4.1 was completely omitted.
10. The verified omission rate across 1,068 independent tracking indices demonstrates a widespread, systemic custom, policy, and practice within the clerk’s infrastructure that effectively deprives individuals of standard procedural due process under color of law.

#### Count C: Consumer Reporting Agency Violations (FCRA)
11. Defendant LexisNexis Risk Solutions willfully disseminated inaccurate public record profiles concerning Plaintiff, specifically reporting a mismatching conviction timeline that logged an active conviction date of August 26 for an incident recorded as occurring later on September 10.
12. Defendant Equifax Information Services has consistently violated 15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b) by continuing to publish a $20,000 Fresno County administrative debt line as an active liability on Plaintiff’s credit profile, despite the debt being formally dropped and vacated by judicial order.
13. Despite receiving explicit dispute notices documenting these clerical data errors, both consumer data aggregators failed to conduct a reasonable reinvestigation or delete the unverified information within the 30-day statutory window mandated by 15 U.S.C. § 1681i.

### IV. CAUSES OF ACTION

**FIRST CLAIM FOR RELIEF** *(Deprivation of Due Process Under Color of Law — 42 U.S.C. § 1983 Against Mohave County)* 14. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 13. The County’s systemic failure to perform its mandatory ministerial record-keeping duties and its 12-year maintenance of an inaccurate status classification constitutes a custom and policy of deliberate indifference to the constitutional due process rights of individuals.

**SECOND CLAIM FOR RELIEF** *(Failure to Assure Maximum Possible Accuracy — 15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b) Against Equifax and LexisNexis)* 15. Plaintiff realleges paragraphs 1 through 13. Defendants failed to maintain or follow reasonable procedures to ensure the maximum possible accuracy of consumer reports, resulting in severe economic opportunities lost, damage to credit standing, and intense emotional distress.
